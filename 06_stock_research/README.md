# Equity Research — 22 companies · 5 sectors · 3 markets

Every note uses the **same five-step framework**:

> **Is it a good business? × Is the price fair? × Can I hold the risk?**

Each one-page note states (1) the business model and a moat test, (2) three valuation scenarios — bear / base / bull — with the assumptions written out, and (3) the observable trigger that would flip the call. Prices are the research-date snapshot; the asset is the reasoning, not the quote.

## Coverage

- **5 sectors**: internet/software (4), consumer tech & EV (3), semis & hardware (4), resources (1), QSR & sportswear (8), fintech/music (2)
- **3 markets**: Hong Kong (9), A-share (2, incl. A/H dual listings), US (9)
- **Two styles**: multi-method cross-valuation (Tencent's four methods, Zijin's scenario + segment, Lenovo's cross-cycle normal earnings) vs single-framework fast calls (Nike, adidas, the four Chinese sportswear names)

## What the 22 notes share (more valuable than any single one)

1. **Characterise the business before pricing it.** Cyclical-growth names (BYD, SK Hynix, Lenovo) are explicitly *not* valued as compounding machines; platforms (Tencent, Google) are split between core and investments.
2. **Three scenarios with the assumptions written out.** No single-point target price — each bear/base/bull case states what has to be true.
3. **Every call carries a "verdict date".** Oracle's 10 Dec earnings, Lenovo's November results, Xtep's "next two quarters of data". Judgements are falsifiable, not permanent opinions.
4. **Unknowable parts are admitted.** SpaceX's ground-terminal cost curve; Intel needing its two businesses valued separately.
